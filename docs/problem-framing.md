# Problem framing
## Bad situation(s) and domain
I frequently experience anxiety towards ordinary day-to-day tasks that require public interactions and learning on the spot (e.g. navigating an airport for the first time or attending a doctor's appointment). Although most people adapt to unfamiliar situations fairly quickly, I and others with anxiety disorders tend to stress when not given detailed instructions. More precisely, some bad situations include:

- Avoiding tasks that are necessary for health or well-being because the stress of planning and executing those tasks can be more stressful than handling the consequences (e.g. avoiding traveling, missing events or appointments)
- Wasting time trying to compile information about a particular task so that it can be performed without stress. Most ordinary tasks lack good documentation online (either because the task is too basic to be explicitly outlined or because documentation websites are outdated). Online information can also be inaccurate for different parts of the world or even different companies providing the same service

Given this general description of the problem, the domain is very broad: people from all backgrounds that struggle with common tasks, especially those with anxiety disorders. From this broad domain, there are only two stakeholders:

- Individuals that are struggling to navigate systems in which they lack knowledge or power
- Individuals or organizations that hold some sort of administrative power in the aformentioned ordinary tasks. For example, the doctor and hopsital staff that control the appointment booking system. 

Only the first group of stakeholders actively engage with this problem. These stakeholders have a particular short-term goal that would be considered trivial by a majority of people (e.g. booking an appointment), but are impeded by anxiety and ambiguity in instructions.

Critically, the second group of stakeholders does not typically engage in this problem beyond providing enough information for the average person despite being the best suited to provide accurate information. Solutions in this domain must originate from the people actively struggling, as they are the only ones motivated to overcome this uncommon set of problems. Thus, stakeholders defer to collaborative solutions, such as sharing advice on how to navigate situations with friends, family, or people online (see [corroboration and workarounds](##corroboration-and-workarounds)).

## Corroboration and workarounds
It is difficult to gauge how widespread this bad situation is considering the lack of specific research, but we can make an educated guess that they are prevalent given that [31.1% of adults experience an anxiety disorder at some point in their life](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/statistics/any-anxiety-disorder).
More anecdotally, we can look at forums such as [r/Explainlikeimscared](https://www.reddit.com/r/Explainlikeimscared/) that are remedies to this exact situation; on this subreddit, 52,000+ weekly visitors ask questions such as ["How to get blood drawn"](https://www.reddit.com/r/Explainlikeimscared/comments/1w2wq0u/how_to_get_blood_drawn/) and ["Where do I go in Planet Fitness to do my routine without making anyone mad?"](https://www.reddit.com/r/Explainlikeimscared/comments/1vxtjj8/where_do_i_go_in_planet_fitness_to_do_my_routine/) and receive advice from helpful strangers.

While these forums can be effective, they lack specificity in my experience. I often still feel anxious completing some tasks because the relevant web pages or forum threads can't explain the procedures of a particular airport or bus terminal for example. I also tend to have follow-up questions (e.g "what do I do once I reach the train platform?") that are impossible to answer when threads are locked or outdated. I've found that general large-language models like Claude are useful for asking follow-ups, but they lack personalized advice and are often unable to answer questions about particular places or tasks.

From my searches, there are no existing apps or software that attempt to solve this problem beyond posts on social media. This is evidence that the bad situations can be mitigated with low-technology solutions that rely mostly on collaborative effort.
