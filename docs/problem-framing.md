Given this general description of the problem, the domain is very broad: people from all backgrounds that struggle with common tasks, especially those with anxiety disorders.

The only stakeholders engaging with this problem are individuals that are struggling to navigate systems in which they lack knowledge or power. These stakeholders have a particular short-term goal that would be considered trivial by a majority of people (e.g. booking an appointment), but are impeded by anxiety and ambiguity in instructions.

Critically, people that do not usually struggle with common tasks and people with power/knowledge about a particular goal (e.g. the doctor that controls the appointment booking system) are not stakeholders. Solutions in this domain must originate from the people actively struggling, as they are the only ones motivated to overcome this uncommon set of problems. Thus, stakeholders defer to collaborative solutions, such as sharing advice on how to navigate situations with friends, family, or people online (see [corroboration and workarounds](##corroboration-and-workarounds)).

## Corroboration and workarounds
It is difficult to gauge how widespread this bad situation is considering the lack of specific research, but we can make an educated guess that they are prevalent given that [31.1% of adults experience an anxiety disorder at some point in their life](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/statistics/any-anxiety-disorder).
More anecdotally, we can look at forums such as [r/Explainlikeimscared](https://www.reddit.com/r/Explainlikeimscared/) that are remedies to this exact situation; on this subreddit, 52,000+ weekly visitors ask questions such as ["How to get blood drawn"](https://www.reddit.com/r/Explainlikeimscared/comments/1w2wq0u/how_to_get_blood_drawn/) and ["Where do I go in Planet Fitness to do my routine without making anyone mad?"](https://www.reddit.com/r/Explainlikeimscared/comments/1vxtjj8/where_do_i_go_in_planet_fitness_to_do_my_routine/) and receive advice from helpful strangers.

While these forums can be effective, they lack specificity in my experience. I often still feel anxious completing some tasks because the relevant web pages or forum threads can't explain the procedures of a particular airport or bus terminal for example. I also tend to have follow-up questions (e.g "what do I do once I reach the train platform?") that are impossible to answer when threads are locked or outdated. I've found that general large-language models like Claude are useful for asking follow-ups, but they lack personalized advice.
